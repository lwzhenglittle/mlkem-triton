# The `vs 16P` column, measured correctly (2026-11-27)

Generated by `benchmarks/join_16p_table.py` from `bench_primitive.py --batch 65536 --json` and
`bench_16p_barrier.py --all --json benchmarks/cpu_baseline_barrier.json`, both inside one window
on 2026-11-27 (CPU baseline timestamp `12:34:00+0800`, device run minutes later).

Two errors were in the old column, and they have different sizes:

1. **Aggregation.** `bench_cpu.py` summed each child's *minimum* over its own reps, from a pool
   whose children start and finish at different times, so a child's cheapest repetition was often
   taken while some of the other fifteen had already exited. The barrier does one timed round per
   process, all released together, and divides the union of units by the union of the window.
2. **Message shape.** The device hashes 1024-byte messages (`bench_primitive.py --size 1024`) and
   the CPU rival was pointed at `openssl speed -bytes 16384` for the digests and at 1 MiB messages
   for BLAKE3 and Poly1305. Per-message init/final was charged to the device only.

## Why `16P` still is not a clean ceiling

Two estimators are printed by `bench_16p_barrier.py`: `procs16` (total units over the union
window, used above) and `procs16_steady` (each process's own rate, summed). They differ by
1.10x-1.43x on this 4P+8E+4LP part, because a fixed pile of work split sixteen ways waits for the
slowest core. The batch figure is the one quoted: it is the conservative choice for the device and
it is what a "join" costs. Neither is a minimum over repetitions any more.

A third bias remains and is not fixed here: the device's clock state moves 2-4x, and it moved
*within* this session (the same 2^20 transform read 810, 1160 and 602 transforms/s in three runs
twenty minutes apart). Absolute GB/s in any snapshot is therefore worth less than the ratio
measured beside it, which is why the two sides of this table were taken minutes apart and not
days.
